# Business Email Drafting

## When to Use
Requests to draft, rewrite, tighten, or "make better" a business email — cold outreach, post-event networking, post-silence follow-ups, scheduling asks. (Inbound triage lives in email-inbox-triage; sending mechanics in himalaya — this skill is the writing spec.)

## Standing spec (Greg's bar)
- **Crisp**: one context line, short paragraphs, zero filler (no "Hope all is well" padding, no restating the recipient's known situation).
- **One ask per email**, and it must be easy to answer: a specific duration ("20–30 minutes"), a bounded timeframe ("next two or three weeks"), or a calendar link — never open-ended "let me know what works."
- **Low-friction replies**: offer named options the reader can pick in one line ("still pursuing / parked / closed", "coffee near you or a virtual intro"). An explicit easy-out beats silence.
- **No self-deprecating hedging**: cut "I was wondering", "if you have a minute", "thanks in advance", apology-for-bothering tone.
- **Hook = specificity about THEM**: tie the ask to something concrete the recipient said/did (their panel point, product capability) mapped to a concrete project fact (client type, stage, location). Replace vague "synergies" with a named capability-to-pain-point match.
- Signature when Greg signs: Greg, Fairchild Technology Group, LLC.

## Procedure
1. Identify the email's job (get a reply? get a meeting? nudge after silence?) — the ask follows from the job.
2. Rewrite to the standing spec above.
3. When the user asks for "options" or "styles", deliver 2–3 variants labeled by angle — typically **value-first** (recommended), **short/inbox-respectful**, **formal** — each as a subject line + body, each followed by a one-line parenthetical explaining why its hook works. Put the recommended option first.
4. Close with 2–3 practical tips when relevant: reply in the original thread (keeps context), one meeting ask / one timeframe, swap in the most specific recipient-capability ↔ own-project match available.

## Follow-up-after-silence variant
For a nudge to an email that got no reply:
- Reference the prior exchange in one line ("Floating this back up…" / "didn't want my note to disappear into post-conference chaos") — do not recap it.
- Compress the value prop to one or two lines with concrete nouns (project types, locations, product line names).
- End with exactly one scheduling question.

## Pitfalls
- Don't preserve the original's structure when tightening — a rewrite that keeps all three of the original's questions just made the email shorter, not crisper; collapse multiple questions into one response path with named options.
- Don't flatten "in person or video, wherever you are" into two asks — resolve location friction yourself ("happy to come to you").
